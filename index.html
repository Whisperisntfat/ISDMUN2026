Conference structure

Rules of Procedure

The formal guidelines governing debate, motions, questions, voting, resolutions and delegate conduct.

Definition

The set of formal guidelines that govern how a committee conducts debate, makes decisions, raises motions, asks questions, and votes on resolutions.

Purpose

Ensures orderly, fair, and diplomatically rigorous discussions across all delegates.

Standard of Conduct

Demands strict decorum, parliamentary language, and respect for the Chair and fellow delegates.

01

Roll Call & Quorum

The Process: The Chair calls each country's name alphabetically or by roll.

Response Options:

  • Present: Delegates can vote Yes, No, or Abstain on substantive matters.
  • Present and Voting: Delegates must vote either Yes or No; no abstentions are allowed.

Quorum: The minimum number of member states required to be present for the committee to officially open and conduct business.

02

Yielding Remaining Time

After finishing a substantive speech in formal debate, a delegate can yield their leftover time.

  1. Yield to the Chair: Gives leftover time back to the Chair, who decides what happens next.
  2. Yield to Another Delegate: Transfers remaining time to a specific delegate to continue speaking.
  3. Yield to Questions: Opens the floor for other delegates to ask questions about the speech.
03

Moderated Caucus

Definition: A structured, formal debate format focused on a specific sub-topic.

Mechanics:

  • The Chair controls the speaking order.
  • Time per speaker is fixed, typically 30 to 60 seconds.
  • Delegates speak one by one from their seats.

Purpose: To explore particular aspects of the main agenda item and build momentum for ideas.

04

Unmoderated Caucus

Definition: An informal, flexible discussion where delegates can leave their seats and move freely around the room.

Mechanics:

  • No formal speaking order or strict time limit per individual.
  • Total duration is set by the Chair through a passing motion, typically 10 to 20 minutes.

Purpose: To collaborate, negotiate, merge proposals, and draft working papers or resolutions.

05

Points

Questions or clarifications a delegate can raise during debate.

  • Point of Personal Privilege: Addresses physical comfort or audibility.
  • Point of Order: Highlights a procedural error.
  • Point of Inquiry: Asks the Chair a question about committee procedure.
  • Point of Information: Poses a question to a speaker.
06

Motions

A formal request to move the committee forward or take action.

  • Motion for a Moderated Caucus
  • Motion for an Unmoderated Caucus
  • Motion to Close Debate / Move to Voting
07

Draft Resolutions & Structure

Definition: The comprehensive document created by delegates listing concrete ideas and solutions to solve the committee's topic.

  1. Know your topic: Research your nation's foreign policy stance.
  2. Team up: Form coalitions and blocs with other delegates.
  3. Structure: Include the committee name, topic, sponsors and signatories; preambulatory clauses explaining why the issue matters; and operative clauses mandating actionable solutions.
  4. Review: Ensure solutions are realistic, clear, and structurally sound.
08

Amendments Process

Definition: Formal suggestions to change, add, or remove parts of a draft resolution before final voting.

  1. Draft the Amendment: Clearly write the proposed additions, deletions, or alterations.
  2. Get Approval: Submit the text to the Chair to ensure it is in order.
  3. Seek Support: Gather the required sponsors and signatories.
  4. Debate & Vote: The committee discusses only the proposed change before voting.
  5. Incorporate: If passed, the amendment is integrated into the draft resolution.
09

Voting Procedure

Security: Once voting procedure begins, committee doors are locked; no entering or exiting is permitted.

Sequence of Votes:

  1. Amendments are voted on first.
  2. Main draft resolutions are voted on second.

Voting Methods:

  • Placard Vote: Raising country placards to indicate Yes, No, or Abstain.
  • Roll-Call Vote: The Chair calls countries individually if requested.

Threshold: Requires a simple majority to pass and become an official resolution of the conference.
